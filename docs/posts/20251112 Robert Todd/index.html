Two dozen grandchildren gathered and sang, I am trying to be like Jesus.

A boy about 8 or 9 was overcome with emotion and was crying throughout the song.

Perhaps he was thinking of his grandfather, who recently passed away. 13 days after learning about the cancer diagnosis.

Appropriate number considering, he spent his nearly all his adult life trying to emulate that Being, we call Jesus Christ.

He was my neighbor, his home was to the west of the road we shared.

He was born 20 years earlier than me.

And he married, at the same age as when I married Sister K.

However, he had twice as many children than us, 8 instead of 4.

One time, I was looking for a new role and came across a position in the program that he developed.

Sent him my resume, asking for his opinion, whether this role was a good fit.

He sent me two replies.

One was questioning my fit.

Next one said, he reviewed once more and changed his mind and said I would be a good fit.

Always encouraging another person.

On the west side of our home is a tree that was growing too fast.

I decided to chop the top off, not having much experience.

One of the branches fell in a manner I didn’t expect and landed on a metal fence.

Because of the weight of the branch, the fence was bent on impact.

I chalked it up as an experience and moved on.

I didn’t think was fixable.

My neighbor took a look and found a way to reshape it into the original form.

This was all done, while a neighbor was renting that house.

So I was never involved.

Three of his children spoke.

Giving us a glimpse of a life with a father that emulated Jesus Christ.

He would take a side trip to a hardware store, while his daughter was heading to the hospital to deliver a baby.

They not only had daily scripture study and a family prayer at the start of the day, but also have a monthly Personal Priesthood interview. A binder would be kept that tracked the progress or issues for each person.

Last things he shared publicly, after his cancer diagnosis was this,

I am grateful that Heavenly Father trusted me to have this experience, so that I can learn further what Christ went through.

His neighbor, and the former dean of BYU Law School and an associate for 9.5 years1 paid a tribute.

A Faithful Neighbor

Two dozen grandchildren gathered and sang “I Am Trying to Be Like Jesus.”

Among them, a boy—about eight or nine years old—was overcome with emotion, tears covering his face throughout the song.

He was thinking of his grandfather, who had recently passed away—just thirteen days after learning of his cancer diagnosis.

—

A Neighbor and a Friend

He was my neighbor—his home sat just west of the road we shared.

We had been neighbors for 23 years.

He was born twenty years before me, and he had married at the same age as I did.

He and his wife raised eight children—twice as many as Sister K and I.

One time, while I was searching for a new role, I came across a position in the Captstone Program he had developed. I sent him my résumé, asking if he thought the role might be a good fit.

He sent two replies: the first, expressing doubt; the second, after reviewing again, saying he had changed his mind and believed I would be a good fit.

That was him—always encouraging, always believing in others.

An Example of Quiet Service

On the west side of our home stands a tree that once grew too fast for its own good. I decided to trim it, though I lacked experience.

A heavy branch fell in an unexpected way, bending the metal fence below. I assumed it couldn’t be fixed and moved on.

Later, my neighbor noticed it. Without ever mentioning it to me, he found a way to reshape the fence back to its original form.

He did this quietly, while another family was renting that house. I only learned about it later—an act of quiet service, done without recognition.

A Christlike Legacy

During the funeral, three of his children spoke, each offering a glimpse into a home where discipleship was lived daily.

They described a father who took a brief detour to a hardware store—on the way to the hospital where his daughter was about to give birth.

They spoke of mornings that began with scripture study and family prayer, and even monthly personal priesthood interviews.

He kept a binder to track each child’s progress, concerns, and goals—a loving steward of his family’s spiritual growth.

After his diagnosis, he shared this message, the last public one :

“I am grateful that Heavenly Father trusted me to have this experience, so that I can learn further what Christ went through.”

His longtime colleague2 and neighbor—former dean of BYU Law School—paid tribute by describing him through the lens of the Boy Scout Law 3:

A Scout is trustworthy, loyal, helpful, friendly, courteous, kind, obedient, cheerful, thrifty, brave, clean, and reverent.

He embodied each of these.

A Farewell in Faith

As the funeral procession left the chapel, five Model A Fords followed the limousine—cars just like the ones he loved to restore.

One story told how, at age twelve, he bought his first Model A from a butcher at a grocery store. It became a lifelong passion, symbolic of his knack for rebuilding and restoring—not just cars, but people and relationships.

Thank you, Brother Bob, for all you have done and all you have quietly taught.

Showing a glimpse of what celestial life would be. Building up a family in love, learning about and working on all things pertaining to the kingdom.
