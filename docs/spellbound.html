Screen Directors Playhouse: Spellbound

Starring Joseph Cotten and Mercedes McCambridge

Author

NBC Radio – All Star Festival

Introduction

Series: Screen Directors Playhouse
Episode: Spellbound
Stars: Joseph Cotten, Mercedes McCambridge
Host / Narrator: Alfred Hitchcock
Original Sponsor Mentions: Anderson (Analgesic), RCA Victor

Opening Narration – Alfred Hitchcock

Good evening, ladies and gentlemen. This is Alfred Hitchcock.
The prime desire of any motion picture maker is to entertain his audience.
But occasionally an idea is presented to be both entertaining and instructive.
When we achieve such a wedding of qualities, we feel we really have something.

Spellbound was the first motion picture to deal with psychoanalysis—the method by which modern science treats the emotional problems of the sane…

And now the first act of Spellbound…

Cast

  • Dr. Constance Peterson – Mercedes McCambridge
  • Dr. Edwards / John Ballantyne (J.B.) – Joseph Cotten
  • Narrator / Host – Alfred Hitchcock
  • Dr. Murchison
  • Dr. Brulov (Alex)
  • Sheriff / Lieutenant Cooley
  • Supporting Characters – Green Manors medical staff, patients, hotel staff

Act I: Green Manors

Constance Peterson & Dr. Murchison

“Your work is brilliant but lifeless… There is no intuition in it.” —Dr. Murchison

Constance rebuffs Murchison’s attempts to “improve” her as both a doctor and woman. Their discussion ends as she leaves to meet the new chief of staff.

Arrival of “Dr. Edwards”

Constance meets the supposed Dr. Anthony Edwards, newly appointed to Green Manors.

A moment of tension arises when Edwards draws lines with his fork on a white tablecloth—triggering an odd reaction.

The Garms Patient

A patient, Garms, insists:

“I killed my father.”

Edwards becomes fascinated with the case, but later collapses during an emergency.

First Romantic Tension

That night, Constance confronts Edwards about her feelings.

“Something has happened to us… It happens in a moment sometimes.” —Edwards

But mid-embrace, he recoils at the sight of her white robe with dark lines, shuddering.

Soon after, Garms attempts suicide.

Act II: Discovery

Constance discovers a signature mismatch:

Edwards’ handwriting does not match the real Dr. Edwards.
Her patient is an impostor.

He confesses:

“I am not Dr. Edwards… I don’t know who I am.”

He believes he murdered the real Edwards. Constance, seeing symptoms of amnesia and guilt complex, refuses to abandon him.

He flees in the night, leaving a note:

“I love you. Tell the police I am at the Empire State Hotel.”

Constance follows.

Act III: New York & Escape

Empire State Hotel

Constance finds him registered as John Brown (J.B.).
She insists on helping him recover his memory.

He remembers:

  • A cigarette case with initials J.B.
  • A sudden headache upon seeing it
  • Nothing before arriving at Green Manors

Constance pushes him:

“Your guilt is imagined. It comes from childhood.”

But he argues logically that he must have been with Edwards when he died.

Newspaper Break

A headline appears:

“Police Hunt Dr. Constance Peterson — Believed Aiding Madman”

They flee to Rochester to visit Dr. Brulov, Constance’s mentor.

Act IV: Dr. Brulov’s House

Brulov assumes they are newlyweds. He gives them a room with two beds.

A key sequence occurs:

The Color White

The white bedspread terrifies J.B.

Constance forces him to look at it:

“White… dark lines…”

He clutches a straight razor without knowing why.

He suddenly flashes an image:

“An old man… the razor…”

But the memory collapses.

Act V: The Unfolding Memory

During the night, Brulov discovers J.B. holding the razor, murmuring:

“I was the old man…”

Constance insists he is near breakthrough.
